=============================================================
HIDING TRANSMITTER LOCATION — NOTES
(Companion notes to QEKH v5 encrypted radio project)
=============================================================

CORE TRUTH
----------
Encryption hides WHAT you say, not WHERE you are.
QEKH v5 protects content perfectly, but radio direction finding
(RDF) does not need to read your message at all. It works on the
raw physics of your transmission. You need a second layer of
defense against a completely different threat model.


HOW THEY LOCATE YOU (know your enemy)
-------------------------------------
1. AoA triangulation (angle of arrival):
   Two or more receiving stations measure the bearing your signal
   arrives from (rotating loops, phased arrays, Doppler antennas).
   Where the bearing lines cross is you. Accuracy improves with
   distance between their stations and number of measurements.

2. TDOA (time difference of arrival):
   Multiple receivers timestamp your signal's arrival; the
   differences place you on hyperbolas. Works on skywave
   (ionosphere-bounced) signals. This is how state actors
   geolocate HF transmitters, at continental scale. This is
   exactly the "bounces off the ionosphere" scenario.

3. Signal analysis:
   Even encrypted, your transmission has a fingerprint:
   modulation type, bandwidth, duration, schedule, and even
   transmitter-specific hardware imperfections (RF fingerprinting).
   Regular habits alone ("every night at 9pm") shrink the
   search area.


WHAT ACTUALLY WORKS (roughly in order of effectiveness)
-------------------------------------------------------

1. TRANSMIT LESS — this is ~80% of the defense.
   RDF needs signal-on-air time. Every second you transmit is
   measurement opportunity.
   - Compress everything, use BURST TRANSMISSION: record offline,
     transmit the whole thing in the shortest possible burst
     (sub-second if the link allows), then go dark.
   - Transmit at RANDOM, IRREGULAR times, never on a schedule.
   - One transmission, one location — NEVER transmit twice from
     the same place.

2. SEPARATE THE TRANSMITTER FROM YOU.
   The big one, purely offline-compatible:
   - Remote/unattended transmitter: leave a cheap ESP32+radio
     node at a distant location, trigger it over a short-range
     hop or timer (dead-man schedule), be nowhere near it when
     it fires. RDF finds the node, not you. Cost: a $15 device.
   - Store-and-forward relay: you -> short-range link (BLE/WiFi/
     LoRa at minimum power, meters of range) -> relay node far
     away -> HF transmitter. Your detectable emission is tiny and
     local; the loud emission is somewhere you are not.
   - Moving transmitter: transmit from a vehicle in motion, or
     drop it on a passing bus/train route. A location fix of a
     moving point is stale the moment it is computed.

3. PHYSICS TRICKS (weaker, but additive):
   - Directional antennas (Yagi, corner reflector): point energy
     only where it needs to go. Skywave HF is hard to make truly
     directional for the ionosphere path, but it slashes
     GROUND-WAVE leakage to nearby receivers.
   - NVIS (near-vertical incidence skywave): shoot nearly
     straight up; signal comes down in a ~300-500 km circle with
     almost no ground wave. Harder for distant ground stations to
     catch — good if your intended receiver is regional and your
     adversary is local, bad if they are regional too.
   - Minimum necessary power: every dB you cut shrinks the radius
     where you are detectable. Use just enough for your link
     budget.
   - Frequency agility (FHSS): hop across many channels on a
     pseudo-random schedule only your receiver knows (seed it
     from your shared key — you already have HKDF!). Listeners
     with a single narrowband receiver miss most of your signal;
     a wideband adversary still catches it, but you have raised
     their cost.

4. DON'T BE AT "HOME."
   The single most common failure: people transmit from their
   residence. Even perfect burst discipline fails eventually if
   every fix points at the same house. Rotate locations, and make
   none of them traceable to your identity.


WHAT DOES NOT WORK
------------------
- Encryption as location protection — irrelevant to RDF.
- Hiding in noise / LPI (DSSS spread spectrum below the noise
  floor): real militaries use this, but on HF it is extremely
  hard with hobbyist hardware, and a wideband recording adversary
  can still integrate and find you. Do not rely on it.
- Decoys (fake transmitters elsewhere): costs an adversary time
  but does not survive pattern analysis if your real habits leak.


THE REALISTIC, HONEST CEILING
-----------------------------
Against a CASUAL/LOCAL adversary (someone with an SDR and a
directional antenna):
  bursts + min power + random schedule + directional antenna
  make you very hard to find.

Against a STATE-LEVEL adversary with a TDOA receiver network:
  if you transmit skywave HF regularly, they WILL eventually
  geolocate the transmitter. Full stop. Your only real defenses
  are the architectural ones: REMOTE, DISPOSABLE, MOBILE,
  ONE-TIME transmitters — so the location they find is a $15 box,
  not you. Physics does not negotiate; you route around it by
  making the found location worthless.


THE PATTERN TO REMEMBER
-----------------------
The strongest protections are ARCHITECTURAL and BEHAVIORAL
(remote nodes, bursts, rotation), not cryptographic.
Math and physics help at the margins (FHSS schedules from HKDF
keys, directional gain, NVIS geometry, link-budget-driven power
minimization), but the winning move is:

  When the signal IS caught — and it will be — its origin
  tells them nothing about you.
